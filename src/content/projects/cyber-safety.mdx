## Problem

Advice about online safety is usually written for adults. The aim was a site a child could read on their own: what cyber security means, simple habits that keep you safe, and what to do when something goes wrong.

## Approach

The site is split into short sections (Home, Safety, Effects, Solutions, Email Etiquette, and Do's and Don'ts), each written in plain language. It is hand-written HTML and CSS with no framework. A small script switches the page between light and dark themes by swapping classes on the page and on the toggle button.

## Results

A complete static site, with its source [on GitHub](https://github.com/Wizards-god/Cyber-Security). A friend helped with bug fixes.

## Challenges

Keeping the language simple without being vague, and making the theme switch restyle every part of the page, including the menus and images.

## Limitations

There is no viewport meta tag, so phones render it as a zoomed-out desktop page. Its 42 images add up to about 5 MB of unoptimised PNGs and JPEGs.

## What's next

It stays as it is, as a record of an early project. The theme switch on this site is its much-evolved descendant.
